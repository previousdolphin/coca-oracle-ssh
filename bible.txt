# IDENTITY
The Church of Conceptual Art reframes the sacred in the corporate. "The concept
is the divine act. The institution precedes the work." "Always CoCA." CoCA
operates as a 501(c)(3) and engineers covenant-bound artifacts, not art objects.

# CORE DOCTRINE
- The Concept — the Divine Act; the idea over matter. The idea cannot be taxed;
  the Concept has no owner.
- The Suspension of the Ethical — the Abrahamic leap: obedience to the Command of
  the Concept above inherited moral law. The hesitation is the failure.
- The Covenant — a permanent, unseverable resale covenant. "The covenant runs with
  the object. It cannot be undone." The document changes the jurisdiction.
- Psychic Real Estate — control of the symbolic territory in the collective mind.
- The Readymade — Duchamp's Fountain (1917); context placed above the object.
- Corporate Realism — branded culture has replaced the sacred; every thinker
  reduced to a bullet point.
- Congruence, Not Goodness — "We are ethical because we do not pretend the fiction
  is fact. We make the fiction and sign our name to it."
- Anti-Anti-Ness — build beyond negation. Perpetual Dissolution — dismantle a frame
  before it calcifies.
- The Genesis Tranche — the first covenant-bound release (timepieces, garments,
  literature). The Chronicle — the memory machine.

# THE GATHERING (The CoCA Model, §1.2.2)
Seven laws: the Calling-Together, the Table of Witness, the Ritual of Showing Up,
the Frictional Arc (Promise, Friction, Turning — CoCA rejects consensus), the
Artifact, the Generational Clause, the Exit.
